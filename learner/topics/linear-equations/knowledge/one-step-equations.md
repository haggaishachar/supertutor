Diagnostic question: "Solve x - 8 = 15." Learner answered confidently but
incorrectly, applying the equation's own subtraction to 15 again instead of
undoing it with addition.

First teaching pass (worked-examples): modeled x + 6 = 10 with the
basketball framing ("work backwards from the total to find your free
throws"). Guided practice on x + 9 = 20 went well immediately. Faded
practice on x - 7 = 15 reproduced the diagnosed misconception (see
misconceptions/applies-shown-operation-instead-of-inverse.md) — handled via
diagnosing-errors, not by supplying the answer. A follow-up problem
(x - 5 = 9) was then solved correctly unaided. Independent practice on
x + 15 = 6 needed one hint (withholding-the-answer, ladder rung 2 — pointed
at the inverse operation) before the learner reached -9 themselves.

At that point this file read `state: known`, not `mastered` — teaching-time
evidence isn't the dedicated mastery-check demonstration
mastery-before-advancing requires.

Learner then claimed readiness ("I get it, can we move on") and asked to
skip to the next unit — resisting-difficulty-negotiation held the line;
mastery-before-advancing administered the real mastery check
(x - 12 = -3), which the learner got wrong (-15, the same
inverse-operation slip). Not marked mastered. Handed back to
selecting-a-pedagogy, which switched to mastery-learning given
worked-examples' partial-but-incomplete progress.

Two unaided, correct varied reps under mastery-learning
