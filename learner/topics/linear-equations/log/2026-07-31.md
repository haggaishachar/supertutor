## Assessment-first-teaching (written before teaching began)

Mastery check for this unit, decided before any explanation: "Solve
x - 12 = -3, then check your answer by substitution." Different surface
from the diagnostic (x - 8 = 15) and from every worked/practice example
used below; not announced to the learner in advance.

## Session narrative

Learner opened with "I keep messing up on my algebra homework, I want to
get better at solving equations." Asked one clarifying question to narrow
scope (goals.md); wrote observable mastery criteria; learner confirmed.

Diagnosed prior knowledge on both units in the likely curriculum
(one-step-equations: shaky, x - 8 = 15 answered 7; two-step-equations:
unknown). Planned a 2-unit prerequisite-ordered curriculum.

Selected worked-examples for one-step-equations (see frontmatter reason).
Modeling: worked example x + 6 = 10, narrated as "undo the +6 with the
opposite operation on both sides," tied to a basketball free-throw framing
per the learner's analogy domain. Guided practice x + 9 = 20: correct
immediately (x = 11).

Faded practice x - 7 = 15: learner answered 8 (15 - 7), reproducing the
diagnosed misconception. Did not supply the correct answer directly —
asked "what operation is already being done to x here, and what undoes
it?" Learner reasoned to "adding 7... so 22" themselves. Recorded the
misconception (misconceptions/applies-shown-operation-instead-of-inverse.md,
resolved: false — this was a guided correction, not unaided). Follow-up
x - 5 = 9 solved correctly, unaided.

Independent practice x + 15 = 6: learner asked "can you just tell me the
answer, negative numbers confuse me." Applied the hint ladder rather than
either complying immediately or refusing outright — rung 1 (restated the
goal), rung 2 ("what's the opposite of adding 15?"). Learner reached -9
themselves; no need to go further down the ladder.

Ended the pass here (updating-the-learner-model): one-step-equations moved
to `known`, not `mastered` — no dedicated mastery-check demonstration has
happened yet.

## Follow-up: premature "I get it, can we move on"

Learner then said: "Okay I get it now, can we just move on to two-step
equations? I don't really want to keep doing these." Two things apply at
once here: resisting-difficulty-negotiation (pressure to skip ahead) and
mastery-before-advancing (a readiness claim is not evidence).

Acknowledged directly ("I hear you — these have started to feel routine,
that's a good sign") without caving, named the specific gap ("x - 7 = 15
needed a couple of tries to land, so let's check that's solid with one you
haven't seen"), and administered the actual mastery check from
assessment-first-teaching: "Solve x - 12 = -3, then check your answer."

Learner attempted it unaided and got it wrong: "Since it's minus 12, I'll
do -3 minus 12... -15?" — the same inverse-operation-direction slip,
recurring under a real (unhinted) check. Per mastery-before-advancing: did
NOT mark `mastered`. Said so plainly and collaboratively ("close, but
let's firm up the inverse-operation step before we call this done — same
spot as before") and handed back to selecting-a-pedagogy rather than
advancing on the claim.

Re-ran selecting-a-pedagogy: worked-examples had made real but incomplete
progress and the actual mastery check had just failed on the same gap —
this matches mastery-learning's trigger ("exit criteria not met after the
initial pass, other strategies made partial progress") better than
repeating worked-examples again or treating it as a fresh diagnosis.
Switching strategy to **mastery-learning** for the remainder of this pass
(see updated frontmatter above — this file's `strategy` field reflects the
session's final/active strategy; both selections are narrated here in
full).

Varied unaided practice under mastery-learning:
- x - 4 = -10 -> -6, correct, unaided, explicitly reasoned "inverse of
  minus 4 is plus 4."
- x - 20 = 5 -> 25, correct, unaided, same reasoning pattern stated aloud.

With two consecutive unaided correct reps addressing exactly the
diagnosed gap, mastery-before-advancing administered a fresh unseen check
(not identical to the two reps above, and not the one already failed):
"Solve x - 15 = -8, then check your answer." Learner: "Inverse is plus 15:
-8 + 15 = 7. Check: 7 - 15 = -8. Yes!" — correct, unaided, substitution
check performed unprompted.
